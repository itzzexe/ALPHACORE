# Commercial licence

AlphaCore is published under the [AGPL-3.0](LICENSE). For most people that is
the end of it: use it, change it, run it, sell what you build with it.

The AGPL asks one thing in return. If you run a modified copy **as a service
other people reach over a network**, the people using it are entitled to your
modified source. Not your data, not the rest of your company — the source of the
thing they are using.

Some organisations cannot accept that. A commercial licence exists for them.

## Which one do you need?

**The AGPL is enough if you are:**

- running it inside your own company, however heavily modified — the AGPL's
  network clause is about people outside your organisation
- building on it and publishing your changes
- evaluating it, learning from it, or writing about it
- selling consulting, hosting or support around an unmodified copy

**A commercial licence is for you if you want to:**

- offer a modified AlphaCore as a product or a hosted service **without**
  publishing your modifications
- embed it in a closed-source product you distribute
- ship it to a client under terms that forbid copyleft
- have a warranty, an indemnity, or a support commitment — the AGPL offers none
  of these, deliberately

## What a commercial licence gives you

- the same code, without the obligation to publish your modifications
- the right to sublicense it inside a product you distribute
- a named contact and an agreed response time
- optional: priority on a feature, or a private branch maintained for you

## Getting one

Open an issue titled **"Commercial licence enquiry"**, or write to the address
in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), with:

- who you are and what you are building
- whether you are distributing it or hosting it
- roughly how many people will use it

Pricing depends on all three, and is not published because a licence for one
person inside a consultancy and a licence for a platform reselling this to
thousands are not the same thing. You will get a straight answer quickly,
including "the AGPL already covers you, keep your money" — which is the honest
answer more often than not.

---

**Why this is offered at all.** Dual licensing is what lets the code stay
genuinely open — anybody may read it, run it, fork it and improve it — while
still making it possible for the work to be paid for. The alternative most
projects reach for is to keep the good parts closed, and that is a worse deal
for everybody reading this.

Contributions are accepted under the [contributor licence](CLA.md), which is
what makes both licences possible at once.
