# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Disagree about the code as sharply as you
like; do not make it about the person.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the work.** "This is wrong because X" is welcome and useful.
  "You are careless" is not the same sentence and does not belong here.
- **Assume competence.** People arrive knowing different things. Somebody asking
  a basic question is somebody learning, not somebody wasting your time.
- **Say what you mean.** Vague disapproval is harder to act on than a blunt
  objection with a reason attached.
- **Take correction well.** Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of working
  in the open. So is saying so and moving on.
- **English or Arabic**, whichever you think in. Nobody is judged on their
  second language.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or hostility toward somebody rather than their
  argument.
- Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private messages that started here.
- Demeaning anybody for who they are: nationality, religion, gender, age,
  disability, background, or how much they already know.
- Publishing somebody's private information.
- Persistently derailing a discussion after being asked to stop.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code comments — and anywhere somebody is representing the project.

## Reporting

Write to the address in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). A report is read by the
maintainer and nobody else, and the person who reported it is not named to the
person reported without their agreement.

You will get a reply. If a report concerns the maintainer, say so and it will be
handled by asking a neutral third party to look at it — a project with one
maintainer cannot pretend otherwise, and pretending is worse than admitting it.

## What happens

Depending on what happened and whether it repeats: a private word, a public
correction, having a comment removed, or being blocked from the repository.
Deliberate cruelty gets the last one first.

Decisions are explained to the person they affect. There is no appeal process
worth writing down for a project this size; there is a maintainer who will
listen if you think they got it wrong.
